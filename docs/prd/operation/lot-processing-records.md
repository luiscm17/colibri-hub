# Functional Records - Lot Processing

> **Chained from:** [Lot Processing](./lot-processing.md)
> **Related:** [Operation Overview](./overview.md), [Warehouse Finished Product](../warehouse/finished-product.md), and [Access Control](../access-control.md)

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the functional records of Lot Processing. It bridges:

- the business PRD;
- subsequent domain design;
- future data modeling;
- forms, validations, dashboards, queue projections, and lot detail;
- permission-sensitive data projections.

It defines what the business records, at what granularity, who owns the
information, and how it contributes to one lot history. Technical representation
belongs in derived specifications.

---

## 2. Identity and Context Rules

### 2.1 One business lot

Warehouse creates a Finished Product requirement before productive processing.
The requirement contains the unique `lot_code`, target title, required color,
client or destination, and applicable specifications.

Operation creates or resolves one `Production Identity` for that requirement.
The Production Identity is the Operation representation of the same business
lot. It is not defined by Warehouse and does not create another physical product
or parallel code.

The contextual relationship is:

```text
Warehouse Finished Product  1:1  Operation Production Identity
                         same unique lot_code
```

### 2.2 Physical assembly

The requirement and business identity exist before Inventory assembles the
physical set of skeins. Inventory assembly starts the productive stage history
under the existing Production Identity and `lot_code`; it does not create a new
business lot or identifier.

### 2.3 Context-owned records

- Warehouse owns requirement, reception, availability, custody, dispatch, and
  return facts.
- Operation owns Production Identity, stage interventions, stage waste,
  Operation quality, release for reception, and issue responses.
- A unified detail may project authorized facts from both contexts, but neither
  context overwrites records owned by the other.

---

## 3. Cross-Cutting Record Model

### 3.1 Interventions, not document pairs

The process maintains one continuous lot history. A stage intervention does not
create independent delivery and receipt documents. Instead, it creates a
specialized stage record containing, as applicable:

1. inherited or reference data;
2. data verified locally by the stage;
3. new data generated by the stage;
4. observations, incidents, waste, and exit condition;
5. automatic technical and audit data.

### 3.2 Granularity

A stage may have multiple legitimate intervention records for the same lot,
business date, or shift, including records by different users or at different
times. Business date, shift, actors, and timestamps describe history; they are
not uniqueness keys.

Advancement depends on completion of the prior stage, not on the existence of
exactly one intervention record. The stage sequence remains a business rule
regardless of how the records are represented technically.

### 3.3 Business and system time

Every intervention distinguishes:

- **business date:** the operational calendar date entered by the responsible
  user;
- **shift:** the operational shift selected for the intervention;
- **record timestamp:** the exact date and time captured automatically by the
  system.

These values do not represent the same event and must not be conflated. The
current model does not store physical entry-and-exit timestamp pairs per stage
or calculate physical duration. That requirement remains deferred until the
business defines the measured events, capture method, and intended use.

### 3.4 Current recorder and authorization

The **current recorder** states which business actor performs the registration
today. It is not a hardcoded role or permission source.

Authorization comes from effective permissions that combine a general action
with an explicit scope. `Read`, `Write`, `Edit`, and `Edit Outside the
Operational Window` are independent. Shift, actor name, record ownership, and
page visibility do not grant permission.

### 3.5 Permission-sensitive projection

Lot Processing views separate:

- **transversal lot data:** minimum information required to identify and follow
  the lot in the authorized queue or detail;
- **stage-specific technical data:** internal fields owned by a particular
  stage or responsibility;
- **action controls:** registration and correction operations permitted by the
  user's effective actions.

Stage-specific information must not be disclosed when the user lacks the
corresponding `Read`. Interface hiding alone is not an authorization control.

---

## 4. Main Record Family

The principal record family is the **lot stage intervention record**.

Each intervention contributes an immutable historical fact, subject only to
audited correction. Depending on the stage, it may contain:

- entry or inherited data;
- locally verified data;
- technical process data;
- stage waste;
- observations and categorized incidents;
- exit condition toward the next stage;
- completion markers;
- business and system time;
- actor and correction audit information.

Authorized transversal consultation may consolidate waste information without
replacing each stage's own record or transferring ownership to another stage.

## 5. Records by Stage

### 5.1 Inventory - Physical lot assembly

- **Represents:** assembly of the physical set of skeins under an existing
  Production Identity.
- **Granularity:** one Inventory history record per legitimate assembly
  intervention; multiple records may belong to the same lot.

#### Inherited or reference data

- Warehouse Finished Product requirement reference;
- Operation Production Identity and visible `lot_code`;
- target title;
- required color;
- client or destination;
- order or production specifications.

#### Data verified locally

- availability of skeins of the required title;
- quantity of skeins selected or assembled;
- total assembled weight when measured.

#### Data generated by Inventory

- business date;
- shift;
- individual actor who performs the assembly;
- supervisor in charge;
- quantity of skeins contributed by the intervention;
- assembled weight contributed by the intervention;
- completion or exit condition toward Dyeing.

#### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp;
- authenticated recording user;
- applicable audit metadata.

#### Optional or contextual data

- observations;
- assembly incidents;
- shortages or differences;
- correction reason when applicable.

#### Units

- skein quantity;
- weight in kg when actually measured.

#### Current recorder

- Inventory.

#### Permission behavior

- `Read` in the applicable scope permits consultation of Inventory technical
  fields.
- `Write` permits an authorized assembly intervention.
- Correction requires the applicable `Edit` action and window.

---

### 5.2 Dyeing

- **Represents:** processing of the assembled lot in vats to apply the required
  color.
- **Granularity:** one Dyeing history record per legitimate intervention;
  multiple records may belong to the same lot, including re-dyeing.

#### Inherited or reference data

- Production Identity and visible `lot_code`;
- target title and required color;
- quantity of skeins assembled by Inventory;
- Inventory weight, clearly labeled as inherited when not remeasured.

#### Data verified locally

- quantity of skeins received;
- general consistency with the expected lot;
- entry weight only when Dyeing actually measures it.

If Dyeing does not have its own scale, the system must not simulate a
measurement. It must distinguish inherited information from locally measured
or verified data.

#### Data generated by Dyeing

- business date;
- shift;
- individual stage actor;
- supervisor in charge;
- material or process reference when applicable;
- temperature;
- vat number;
- re-dyeing indicator;
- exit condition toward Drying.

#### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp;
- authenticated recording user;
- applicable audit metadata.

#### Stage waste

- quantity or weight when actually measured;
- cause;
- observations.

#### Optional or contextual data

- operational observations;
- temperature incidents;
- incorrect or contaminated vat;
- re-dyeing details.

#### Units

- skeins;
- kg when an actual measurement exists;
- temperature in the approved unit.

#### Current recorder

- Dyeing Personnel.

#### Permission behavior

- Stage-specific `Read` governs Dyeing technical fields.
- Stage-specific `Write` governs intervention registration.
- Correction requires the applicable `Edit` action and window.

---

### 5.3 Drying

- **Represents:** moisture removal after Dyeing.
- **Granularity:** one Drying history record per legitimate intervention;
  multiple records may belong to the same lot.

#### Inherited or reference data

- Production Identity and visible `lot_code`;
- skein quantity;
- relevant authorized Dyeing information.

#### Data verified locally

- quantity of skeins received;
- general condition of the lot;
- weight only when actually measured.

#### Data generated by Drying

- business date;
- shift;
- individual stage actor;
- supervisor in charge;
- actual entry weight when measured;
- exit condition toward Winding or Ball Winding.

#### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp;
- authenticated recording user;
- applicable audit metadata.

#### Stage waste

- quantity or weight when applicable;
- cause;
- observations.

#### Optional or contextual data

- excessive moisture;
- process incidents;
- observations.

#### Units

- skeins;
- kg when an actual measurement exists.

#### Current recorder

- Dyeing Personnel.

#### Permission behavior

- Stage-specific effective permissions govern consultation, registration, and
  correction independently.

---

### 5.4 Winding or Ball Winding

- **Represents:** conversion of skeins to cones or yarn balls.
- **Granularity:** one Winding or Ball Winding history record per legitimate
  intervention; multiple records may belong to the same lot.

#### Inherited or reference data

- Production Identity and visible `lot_code`;
- quantity of skeins from Drying;
- authorized lot-condition information.

#### Data verified locally

- quantity of skeins received;
- general consistency with the Drying output.

#### Data generated by the stage

- business date;
- shift;
- individual stage actor;
- supervisor in charge;
- process variant: Winding or Ball Winding;
- quantity of cones or balls produced;
- exit condition toward Bagging.

#### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp;
- authenticated recording user;
- applicable audit metadata.

#### Stage waste

- quantity or weight actually measured;
- cause;
- observations.

#### Optional or contextual data

- equipment incidents;
- product damage;
- observations.

#### Units

- input skeins;
- output cones or balls;
- kg when applicable to measured waste.

#### Current recorder

- Actor currently assigned to Winding or Ball Winding. This may currently be
  the same person who handles Bagging.

#### Permission behavior

- Current assignment does not grant access. Effective stage permissions govern
  consultation, registration, and correction.

---

### 5.5 Bagging

- **Represents:** packaging of the completed physical product before Quality.
- **Granularity:** one Bagging history record per legitimate intervention;
  multiple records may belong to the same lot.

#### Inherited or reference data

- Production Identity and visible `lot_code`;
- quantity of cones or balls from the preceding stage;
- relevant authorized prior-stage information.

#### Data verified locally

- quantity of cones or balls received;
- lot consistency for packaging.

#### Data generated by Bagging

- business date;
- shift;
- individual stage actor;
- supervisor in charge;
- quantity of bags used;
- quantity of cones or balls per bag;
- exit condition toward Quality.

#### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp;
- authenticated recording user;
- applicable audit metadata.

#### Stage waste

- quantity or weight generated in Bagging when measured;
- cause;
- observations.

#### Optional or contextual data

- label or data-sheet incidents;
- packaging differences;
- observations.

#### Units

- bags;
- cones;
- balls;
- kg when applicable to measured waste.

#### Current recorder

- Packaging.

#### Permission behavior

- Effective stage permissions govern consultation, registration, and
  correction independently.

---

### 5.6 Quality

- **Represents:** final Operation evaluation of the completed lot before the
  finished-product handoff.
- **Granularity:** one Quality history record per legitimate inspection
  intervention; multiple inspection records may belong to the same lot.

#### Inherited or reference data

- Production Identity and visible `lot_code`;
- complete authorized lot history;
- relevant Bagging data;
- original Warehouse requirement data needed for conformity evaluation.

#### Data verified locally

- actual condition of the completed product;
- visible and internal defects;
- correspondence between the requested, produced, and deliverable product.

#### Data generated by Quality

- inspection business date;
- inspection shift;
- individual Quality actor;
- supervisor in charge;
- visible defects;
- internal defects;
- Operation-owned quality state at handoff;
- special nomenclature when applicable;
- delivery conditions to Warehouse.

#### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp;
- authenticated recording user;
- audit metadata.

#### Optional or contextual data

- additional observations;
- special nomenclature under current policy;
- details of Operation decisions before handoff.

#### Units

- catalogs, categories, and descriptions are primary;
- quantitative fields are included only when the approved Quality process
  requires them.

#### Current recorder

- Quality Control.

#### Permission behavior

- Effective permissions govern Quality consultation, intervention recording,
  and correction.

#### Closure boundary

Quality records the product state and delivery conditions. It does not classify
Warehouse inventory, define Warehouse availability, or decide the final
commercial disposition.

---

### 5.7 Finished-product handoff records

The finished-product handoff begins with one release for reception after the
final productive stage is complete. The release records:

- the same Production Identity and visible lot code;
- the responsible Operation actor;
- the exact time of release;
- the applicable quality state and delivery conditions;
- the fact that the handoff is pending Warehouse verification.

The release is named for the business act, not for the position or section that
currently performs it. Quality Control is the current recorder only while that
responsibility remains assigned by business policy.

If Warehouse finds a discrepancy before reception, it records a handoff issue
containing:

- a clear description of the discrepancy;
- the Warehouse actor who reported it;
- the exact reporting time;
- supporting evidence when required by business policy.

The issue places the handoff in resolution required. Operation then records an
issue response containing:

- the correction, remedy, or clarification performed;
- the Operation actor who responded;
- the exact response time;
- supporting evidence when required by business policy.

An issue response returns the same handoff to pending verification. It does not
confirm that Warehouse considers the issue resolved and does not create another
release. Warehouse performs another verification and either records reception
or records another issue. All issues and responses are append-only and remain
visible as one chronological handoff history.

Effective permissions govern release for reception, issue consultation, issue
response, and correction independently. The general authorization action does
not rename any of these business acts.

## 6. Transversal Query Records

### 6.1 Lot queue projection

The lot queue is a read projection, not another source record. It combines only
the information the user may consult.

Minimum transversal fields may include:

- `lot_code`;
- target title;
- lifecycle phase or current stage;
- current responsible area or business actor when applicable;
- last update timestamp.

Stage-specific technical fields are included only with the corresponding
effective `Read`. The queue does not duplicate or take ownership of stage
records.

### 6.2 Lot detail projection

The contextual detail assembles one continuous history from:

- authorized Warehouse requirement data;
- Production Identity reference;
- authorized stage interventions;
- authorized waste and Quality facts;
- corrections and audit data allowed for consultation;
- Warehouse handoff state.

An unauthorized stage may be represented only by the transversal state needed
for continuity. Its internal fields must not be returned.

### 6.3 Dashboard projection

The dashboard aggregates authorized Lot Processing data and may be filtered by
date, shift, stage, title, color, or other validated criteria. It does not create
new source records, actions, or scopes.

## 7. Cross-Cutting Record Rules

1. **Existing requirement:** Lot Processing requires a Warehouse Finished
   Product requirement with a unique `lot_code`.
2. **Operation-owned representation:** Production Identity is created or
   resolved by Operation one to one from that requirement.
3. **Physical assembly under existing identity:** Inventory assembles the
   physical set without creating another business lot or code.
4. **Single history:** Each intervention contributes to the same lot history;
   it is not modeled as independent delivery and receipt pairs.
5. **Inherited versus verified:** Records must distinguish prior-context or
   prior-stage information from facts locally verified or measured.
6. **No simulated measurement:** A stage must not claim a local value when no
   corresponding measurement occurred.
7. **Business time versus system time:** Business date and shift remain separate
   from automatic timestamps.
8. **Multiple interventions:** Date, shift, actor, and timestamp do not impose
   one record per stage.
9. **Sequential validation:** A later-stage intervention requires prior-stage
   completion and remains a business invariant across every interaction.
10. **Correction with audit:** Every correction preserves actor, timestamp,
    reason, prior values, and resulting values.
11. **Operational window:** Ordinary corrections require `Edit` and compliance
    with the owning context's window.
12. **Exceptional correction:** Corrections after the window require `Edit
    Outside the Operational Window` in the owning scope.
13. **Single release:** Only one release for reception starts the
    finished-product handoff.
14. **Issue before reception:** Warehouse records a handoff issue instead of
    reception when a discrepancy requires correction, remedy, or clarification.
15. **Response without restart:** An Operation issue response returns the same
    handoff to pending verification and does not create another release.
16. **Repeatable coordination:** Issue and response cycles may repeat until
    Warehouse records reception.
17. **Reception completes the handoff:** Only Warehouse reception of the same
    Finished Product completes the handoff; issues and responses do not.
18. **Context ownership:** Warehouse and Operation never overwrite each other's
    source records.
19. **Configurable permissions:** Current recorders and actors do not define
    fixed authorization.
20. **Enforced visibility:** Unauthorized stage-specific information is not
    disclosed.
21. **Independent actions:** `Write` does not imply `Read`, and record ownership
    does not imply correction rights.

## 8. Documentation Boundary

This document defines the business meaning, ownership, granularity, history,
and authorization behavior of Lot Processing records. Technical representation,
interfaces, storage, and component design belong in their corresponding derived
specifications and must not redefine these rules.
