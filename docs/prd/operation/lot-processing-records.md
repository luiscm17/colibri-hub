# Functional Records — Lot Processing

> **Chained from:** `docs/prd/operation/lot-processing.md`
> **Related:** `docs/prd/operation/overview.md`, `docs/prd/access-control.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

This document describes the **functional records** of **Lot Processing**.

Its function is to serve as a bridge between:

- the business PRD
- the subsequent domain design
- the future data modeling
- the design of forms, validations, and screens

It does not define tables, technical columns, or database structure. It defines
what the business needs to record, at what granularity, and with what meaning.

---

## 2. Cross-cutting usage rule

The records described here reflect the current expected operation.

- The **current recorder** indicates who enters that data today.
- That assignment **is not rigid**: it can change by access policy.
- The process maintains a **single lot history**.

Each intervention does not create separate "delivery" and "receipt" documents.
Instead, it creates one specialized stage record containing:

1. **inherited data** from the previous stage or from Warehouse
2. **data that the stage can verify locally**
3. **new data generated by the stage itself**
4. **observations, waste, and exit condition**

Additionally, each stage record must explicitly distinguish between:

- **business date**: the operational date entered by the responsible user
- **shift**: the shift selected by the responsible user
- **record timestamp**: the date and time captured automatically by the system

These three pieces do not represent the same event and must not be confused.

This does not impose one record per lot or stage. A lot may have multiple legitimate interventions in the same stage, business date, or shift by different users or at different moments. Business date, shift, actors, and timestamps are history attributes, not uniqueness keys.

Later-stage interventions require completion of the prior stage. This is enforced in the use-case/domain layer across the specialized stage records; it is not expressed as a cross-table database constraint.

---

## 3. Main record family

In Lot Processing the main record family is:

1. **Lot stage record**

Each intervention contributes a specialized record to the lot's history. A record can include, as applicable:

- entry data
- locally verified data
- technical process data
- stage waste
- observations or incidents
- exit condition toward the next stage

Additionally, the Inventory stage receives and consolidates waste information generated by
other sections, without replacing each stage's own record within the lot history.

## 4. Record by stage

## 4.1 Inventory — Lot assembly

- **What it represents:** physical birth of the lot within the process.
- **Granularity:** one Inventory history record per legitimate assembly intervention; multiple records may belong to the same lot.

### Inherited or reference data

- production identity defined by Warehouse and its visible lot code
- yarn count
- color
- customer or destination
- order specifications

### Data that Inventory verifies locally

- availability of skeins of the required yarn count
- quantity of skeins assembled
- total weight of the assembled lot

### Data generated by the stage

- business date
- shift
- operator who assembles the lot
- supervisor in charge
- final quantity of skeins composing the lot
- total lot weight

### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp

### Optional or contextual data

- observations
- assembly incidents
- detail of shortages or differences
- correction reason if a subsequent edit occurred

### Units or measurement

- skein quantity
- total weight in kg

### Current recorder

- Inventory

### Permissions note

- configurable by access policy

## 4.2 Dyeing

- **What it represents:** processing of the lot in vats to apply color.
- **Granularity:** one Dyeing history record per legitimate intervention; multiple records may belong to the same lot.

### Inherited or reference data

- production identity and visible lot code
- yarn count
- required color
- quantity of skeins assembled by Inventory
- lot weight reported by Inventory

### Data that Dyeing verifies locally

- quantity of skeins received
- general match with what was expected for the lot

> **Important:** Dyeing may not have its own scale. Therefore it must not
> simulate a measurement it does not perform. The system must distinguish between
> **inherited data** and **locally verified data**.

### Data generated by the stage

- business date
- shift
- stage operator
- supervisor in charge
- material or process reference if applicable
- temperature
- vat number
- re-dye yes/no
- exit condition toward Drying

### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp

### Stage waste

- waste quantity or weight if applicable
- cause
- observations

### Optional or contextual data

- operational observations
- temperature incidents
- incorrect or contaminated vat
- re-dyeing detail if it occurred

### Units or measurement

- skeins
- kg when an actual measurement exists
- temperature

### Current recorder

- Dyeing personnel

### Permissions note

- configurable by access policy

### Consolidation note

- stage waste also reaches Inventory

## 4.6 Quality

- **What it represents:** final evaluation of the lot before handoff to Warehouse.
- **Granularity:** one Quality history record per legitimate inspection intervention; multiple records may belong to the same lot. At most one record may carry the singular Quality Send marker for that lot.

### Inherited or reference data

- production identity and visible lot code
- complete lot history
- relevant data from Bagging

### Data that Quality verifies locally

- actual condition of the finished product
- visible and internal defects
- correspondence between what was produced and what is deliverable

### Data generated by the stage

- inspection business date
- inspection shift
- quality operator
- supervisor in charge
- visual defects detected
- internal defects detected
- lot quality state at time of handoff
- delivery conditions to Warehouse

### Quality Send

- Quality Send is the one permitted operational action per lot that records an exact send timestamp and actor, leaves the same Warehouse-defined lot in the intermediate awaiting-Warehouse-receipt phase, and implies completed Quality validation. The pending handoff is that singular marker, not the latest record or a timestamp-ordered selection. It ends only when Warehouse records its one acceptance receipt for the same lot identity.
- The UI may show the business date, but the system persists the exact send timestamp for audit and ordering.
- During this phase Warehouse may leave brief coordination notes. Quality can see and address those notes; they do not mean acceptance and do not create another send.
- It does not create separate permissions for implicit inspect, review, or approve sub-steps.
- Operational actor fields are audit facts, not permission sources.

### Automatic technical data

- system record timestamp

### Optional or contextual data

- special nomenclature per current policy
- additional observations
- detail of decisions made within Operation before handoff

### Units or measurement

- not applicable as a primary unit; uses catalogs, categories, and descriptions

### Current recorder

- Quality Control

### Permissions note

- configurable by access policy

### Closure note

- Quality does not definitively classify inventory nor define the final
  commercial disposition. Quality documents the lot state and the conditions
  under which it is handed off to Warehouse.

## 5. Cross-cutting record rules

1. **Physical birth of the lot:** the lot is physically born in this process,
   when Inventory assembles the set of skeins.
2. **Externally defined identity:** Warehouse defines the production identity
   and its visible lot code; Operation does not create new identities or codes.
3. **Single lot history:** each stage complements the same history with
   new data; it is not modeled as separate delivery/receipt pairs.
4. **Inherited data vs verified data:** the system must clearly distinguish
   what information comes from the previous stage and what information was verified
   or actually measured by the current stage.
5. **Business time vs system time:** each stage must store the business
   date and shift entered by the user, in addition to the record timestamp
   captured automatically by the system.
   The current model does not store pairs of physical entry/exit timestamps nor
   calculates duration per stage; that need is deferred until the business
   defines the physical events, capture, and use of that duration.
6. **Correction with audit:** if a data entry error occurs, the record can
   be corrected with full audit trail.
7. **Operational window:** operational roles can correct within the
   window defined by the current policy.
8. **Subsequent restriction:** outside the operational window, only SysAdmin can
   edit.
9. **Unique intermediate handoff:** after Quality Send, the lot remains in the
   awaiting Warehouse receipt state until Warehouse records its one acceptance
   receipt under the same identity. Brief coordination notes do not constitute
   acceptance and do not restart the send.
10. **Configurable permissions:** the current recorder does not equate to a fixed permission.
11. **History continuity in Warehouse:** upon completing Operation, the lot
    history continues under the same identity in the Warehouse unit.

## 6. How to use this document later

This document should feed, in this order:

1. **domain model**
   - what concepts are entities, events, or value objects
2. **data modeling**
   - what information to persist
   - how to group functional records
   - what conceptual relationships must be maintained
3. **forms and UI**
   - what screens each stage needs
   - what data is mandatory
   - what data is inherited
   - what data must be verified locally
   - what data the user enters and what data the system captures automatically
4. **validation rules**
   - sequence between stages
   - completeness control
   - consistency of weights, quantities, and handoffs

It should not be used directly as a table design. Its function is to close
the functional meaning of the data before technical design.
